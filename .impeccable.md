# TwinGrid Design Context

## Users

**Primary**: Relationship managers and financial advisors reviewing client portfolios. They work in an office setting, often reviewing several portfolios per session. Their job-to-be-done is compressing a multi-hour manual review (overlap, concentration, 5-finger alignment) into minutes, while producing recommendations they can defend to the client and to compliance.

**Secondary**: Investment research (validating 5-finger mappings) and compliance reviewers (sampling the rationale trail). Both need every recommendation traceable to a rule.

**Context of use**: Desk-bound, daylight office, calm, deliberative. Not mobile-first. Not a trading floor. The advisor is not rushed — they are *careful*. This is someone else's money.

## Brand Personality

**Three words**: measured, editorial, decisive.

- **Measured** — the tool speaks quietly, even with strong opinions. No exclamation marks, no gradients to "add energy," no badges competing for attention.
- **Editorial** — information architecture feels like a curated report, not a dashboard. There is a clear voice and point of view in how findings are ordered and written.
- **Decisive** — the tool commits. When it recommends trimming a position, it says so plainly and cites the rule. Hedging language ("you may want to consider…") is banned.

**Emotional goals**:
- On arrival: trust that the analysis is serious.
- Through the flow: increasing clarity, never anxiety.
- At the peak moment (Execute Rebalance): *confident decisiveness*.

## Aesthetic Direction

**Reference territory**: The Economist, Financial Times weekend edition, private-bank quarterly client reports. Editorial, restrained, data-respectful.

**Theme**: Light mode by default (daylight office, prints cleanly for client handoff, compliance-friendly). Dark mode for long review sessions.

**Anti-references**:
- **Enterprise legacy (SAP)** — no gray-on-gray tables, no blue bar charts as a reflex, no personality-free density
- Generic SaaS dashboards — purple-to-blue gradients, neon on dark, glass cards

## Design Principles

1. **Numbers come first.** Typography for figures matters more than hero imagery.
2. **Every recommendation is traceable.** The reasoning behind every action must be visible.
3. **Whitespace is authority.** The interface earns trust by not crowding the advisor.
4. **The peak is the commit.** Execute Rebalance is the moment to raise its voice with quiet conviction.

## Delight Opportunities

**Subtle interactions**: Micro-animations on hover, satisfying button presses, smooth page transitions
**Success celebrations**: When analysis completes, when flags are found, on successful actions
**Loading states**: Helpful loading messages, smooth loading states, skeleton screens
**Easter eggs**: Hidden features, surprising hover reveals, personality in empty states
